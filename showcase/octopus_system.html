Distillation. GroundingDINO + SAM2 and Qwen3-VL-235B are never deployed — they are teachers that label data. The deployed models are a 3.2 M-param segmenter and a 1.7 GB 4-bit caption student that captions a clip in ~3 s on a 16 GB Mac with no GPU.

The hidden cost of a big corpus

The corpus is only 7 dates (one week) of the ~209 available days — every model is footage-diversity-limited. That diagnosis drove a dedicated harvest_stream.py crawler that probes each server video first and early-exits, streaming rather than downloading ~1 TB.

Stage 01 · Detection

A frozen CLIP, a tiny probe, and one
preprocessing insight.

Architecture. CLIP ViT-B/32 (frozen) → a small MLP probe 512 → 256 → 64 → 2. Two classes: hidden (no octopus) vs visible. The CLIP backbone never trains; only the probe does, on cached image features — so retraining is seconds, not hours.

The insight that fixed field performance. CLIP's default CenterCrop is destructive — it drops 33–44% of a 16:9 frame, and the octopus is usually at the edge of the den. Switching to letterbox (pad-to-square, keep the whole frame) was the single change that turned a lab-good/field-bad model into a reliable one. It was preprocessing, not architecture.

Hard-negative mining · the verification lesson

We mined the model's confident false positives as hard negatives — but did not assume they were octopus-free. Of 232 candidates, human review found 166 actually contained the octopus (the model was right) and only 66 were genuine negatives. Training on the unverified 232 gave a higher headline number and a worse model. Always verify labels before you train on them.

Result

Folding the mined IR-noise / reflection negatives in cut the hidden false-positive rate 24% → 3% while holding visible recall at ~0.97.

Stage 02 · Extraction pipeline

Two gates turn a month of video
into a handful of clips.

Per video the extractor makes two 1 fps passes — one for the octopus classifier, one for motion — then slides a non-overlapping 20 s window and keeps it only when both gates fire:

  • Visibility gate — >50% of frames are octopus-visible (p_visible ≥ 0.6).
  • Motion gate — mean absolute changed-pixel fraction ≥ 0.008.

Clips are cut with an ffmpeg byte-range copy (no re-encode) and written to a dated tree, with a resumable JSON index and a processed-video ledger.

The motion-gate bug we don't reintroduce

The original per-video motion was normalized (motion / motion.max()) — so a static video with a flickering IR lamp got normalized up to "motion" and passed. The fix is an absolute changed-pixel fraction, and it masks the ticking-clock timestamp region so the clock isn't counted as movement.

Optimization · single-decode scan

The local pipeline decodes each video once and feeds both the CLIP classifier and the motion detector from that one stream (two parallel decodes are slower — decode is already CPU-bound). Scan time on a 30-min video dropped 219 s → 130 s, byte-identical clip set.

What the VLM taught us about the gate

Running the 235B captioner over 847 "verified" clips, 534 (63%) came back "octopus not present" — almost all Right_Left reflections the detector fires on at p = 1.0. Two takeaways baked into the pipeline: drop Right_Left, and a VLM is a far better presence filter than the detector. This is exactly what the segmentation presence-gate later automates.

Stage 03–04 · Captioning & behavioural extraction

Frontier teacher, pocket-sized student.

Each clip gets a one-sentence caption and a 7-class behaviour label. A large VLM produces the training signal; a distilled 2B model runs the job locally.

Teacher · frames the input

Qwen3-VL 30B / 235B

30B via vLLM on an A100 (Colab); 235B via the OpenRouter API — no GPU needed. Per clip: CLAHE-enhance the dim IR frames → score with the detector → send only the clearest frames.

Student · deployed

Qwen3-VL 2B · QLoRA

LoRA r16/α32, 3,066 train / 392 val, 576 steps. Distils the teacher's captions — caption tokens only, label trained separately.

Stage 05 · Segmentation

Pixel masks — the smallest model
that still works.

Masks buy two things: cleaner presence detection (mask area beats the CLIP gate on reflections) and richer behavioural signal for free (body area = posture spread, octopus-only colour, masked motion). Hard design constraint: the smallest model that still gives good masks.

Teacher · offline auto-labeler

GroundingDINO-tiny → SAM2

Per-frame box → mask over-segments (grabs bright metal tools on IR, bleeds into background on colour). The fix: SAM2 video propagation seeded from GroundingDINO's most-confident frame + largest-blob cleanup + area-continuity. IR tool-bleed 11.8% → 6.5%; colour bleed 15.5% → 5.8%; and reflections are correctly rejected by low seed confidence.

Student · deployed

LR-ASPP · 3.2 M params

A compact segmenter distilled from the teacher masks. Runs on-device; gives a per-frame mask + area fraction. The A100 auto-labelled 1,824 colour clips into 4,412 (image, mask) pairs across 77 videos to train it.

Deployment win · reflection-proof presence gate

Trained with empty-mask negatives, the segmenter becomes a presence detector: AUC 0.86 overall and 0.99 vs reflections — mask area on a reflected human → 0.000. At area ≥ 0.01: 88% present-recall, 0% reflection false-positives. This beats the CLIP gate exactly where it was weakest.

The honest limit

Present-mask pixel IoU plateaus ~0.47 (bar was 0.85) across every architecture, augmentation and +IR variant tried. Diagnosed as a video-diversity gap — only 62 distinct training videos (train IoU 0.68 / val 0.47). It fails by mislocating a right-sized blob. Not fixable by architecture; it needs more distinct verified videos — which is what the footage harvest is for.

Stimulus response · human presence

Human presence nearly doubles movement (motion 0.045 → 0.095) and lifts a transparent arousal index 0.46 → 0.68 — a measurable, repeatable welfare signal extracted with zero manual labelling.

Circadian activity runs ~1–5% overnight, climbs to a 45% peak at 17:00 (a 13:00–19:00 plateau) with a small dawn bump. Colour findings hold only on the colour cameras — colour is camera-gated, and colour change needs the segmentation mask to measure honestly.
