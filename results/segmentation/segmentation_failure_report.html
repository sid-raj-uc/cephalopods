Forensic report · octopus mask segmentation

Why the segmentation masks
came out bad — with proof.

Two results came out of this effort, and only one was bad. The reflection-proof presence gate succeeded; the pixel-precise masks did not. Every number below is extracted from the training and evaluation logs in results/segmentation/; the images are the model's own diagnostic overlays.

✓ Worked · presence gate
AUC 0.86

Overall present-vs-absent · 0.99 vs reflections, 0% reflection false-positives. Deployable.

✗ Bad · mask pixel-IoU
~0.47–0.53

Against an 0.85 target. Coarse body-area only, not precise masks. This is the "bad result."

Proof 1 — every model hits the same ~0.47 ceiling

Nine training runs — a from-scratch size sweep, a pretrained architecture, strong augmentation, extra IR data, negatives, and 2× more diverse footage. Not one gets close to 0.85. Bigger models and more augmentation barely move it → the bottleneck is not the model.

source: results/segmentation/seg_train_{ch8,ch16,ch32,lraspp,aug_*,v2,v3}.log — field "BEST val IoU"

Proof 2 — it's a generalization gap, not underfitting

The model fits the training set fine and fails on unseen videos — the signature of too little data diversity.

Proof 3 — the failure mode is mislocation, not bad shapes

On the ~31% of val frames with IoU < 0.3, ground truth is a normal ~3% octopus and the model predicts a normal ~4% blob — in the wrong place (only ~10% of failures predict empty). The masks are the right size; they're just somewhere else. The overlays make it obvious — a clean hit and a total miss on the same camera:

Proof 4 — the root cause chain, and the one thing that did work

v1 was trained on octopus-present frames only — all 4,412 masks contained an octopus, so the model learned to always emit an octopus-shaped blob, even on an empty tank or a glass reflection. That is exactly why it mislocates, and why it was useless as a presence gate:

Adding 1,388 empty-mask negatives (v3) fixed the presence gate — reflections now emit a zero-area mask (0.99 AUC). But mask IoU only nudged 0.47→0.53, because of Proof 5.

Proof 5 — the new ceiling is teacher-label quality

So we added more diverse footage (the obvious fix for a diversity gap). It didn't work — and the reason is the most important finding here:

  • v4: training videos 77 → 170 (~2× diversity) — but the 392 new clips yielded only 532 mask pairs. GroundingDINO's confidence gate rejected the rest, because real footage is dominated by resting / camouflaged / still octopus and the teacher detector isn't confident on exactly that common case. Mask IoU stayed flat at 0.49.
  • IR (Right_Top): 653 clips → 87 accepted (13%). On greyscale, GroundingDINO is low-confidence and SAM2 over-segments bright metal tools (mask area median 8.5% vs 2.9% on colour). Unusable without the point/negative-prompt fix.
  • Data-quality tax: 504 of 731 Right_Right source clips were 0-byte on disk (a prior extraction bug), correctly rejected by the labeler.

The chain: too few distinct videos → student overfits and mislocates. Add diversity → the teacher can't label the hard (resting/camouflaged) frames that dominate. So both teacher and student are weak on the exact regime that matters. It is a data + teacher-label problem, not an architecture one — which is why the size sweep and augmentation in Proof 1 did nothing.

What would actually fix it

  • Better teacher labels on hard frames — the Phase-0 point/negative-prompt SAM2 recipe, or lower the confidence threshold and human-verify. Highest leverage: it unblocks everything downstream.
  • A small human-verified mask val set (~100–200) — so the IoU number is trustworthy (today it's measured against imperfect teacher masks).
  • More distinct videos — but only after the teacher can label them; diversity you can't label is wasted (the v4 lesson).
  • Compute to re-run the improved teacher recipe at scale.
